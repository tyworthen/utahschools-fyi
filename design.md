# Follow the Money --- Design Specification

## 1. Design Concept

**Project:** Tooele County School Finance Accountability Project\
**Design Direction:** Follow the Money\
**Primary Audience:** Tooele County taxpayers\
**Primary Goals:** Government accountability, taxpayer education,
financial transparency, and evidence-based investigation\
**Tone:** Independent, confident, analytical, accessible, civic-minded,
nonpartisan

### Core Design Idea

The site should feel like a modern investigative data-journalism
publication rather than a school district website, political campaign
site, or generic government portal.

The visual identity should communicate:

-   **Follow the money**
-   **Look at the numbers**
-   **Ask better questions**
-   **Show the evidence**
-   **Let taxpayers reach their own conclusions**

The design can be visually bold, but the underlying presentation must
remain politically neutral. Strong typography, dark backgrounds, bright
data accents, and large charts create energy without using partisan
visual conventions.

# 3. Visual Personality

The design should be:

### Bold

Large headlines and strong visual hierarchy.

### Analytical

Charts, numbers, comparisons, and source references should dominate over
decorative elements.

### Trustworthy

Avoid visual tricks, sensationalism, or manipulative color choices.

### Local

Use Tooele Valley imagery selectively to establish place and community.

### Accessible

A taxpayer who has never read a government budget should be able to
understand the primary message of every page.

### Independent

Do not imitate the official Tooele County School District visual
identity.

# 5. Semantic Colors

Semantic colors must be separated from the brand palette.

## Positive / Favorable

`#15803D`

Use for:

-   Revenue exceeding forecast
-   Savings
-   Declining debt
-   Positive outcome trends

## Warning / Investigate

`#D97706`

Use for:

-   Unusual changes
-   Budget variances
-   Items requiring additional investigation

This is the preferred color for "follow-up question" indicators.

## Negative / Adverse

`#DC2626`

Use only when the underlying metric has an objectively negative meaning.

Examples:

-   Expenditure over budget
-   Declining measured outcome
-   Debt increase

Do not use red simply because the author considers a spending increase
undesirable.

## Informational

`#2563EB`

Use for:

-   Links
-   Secondary charts
-   Informational indicators
-   Neutral comparison data

# 6. Data Visualization Rules

Data visualization is a core component of the identity.

## Principle

**The chart should explain the data, not persuade the reader.**

### Required practices

-   Always label axes.
-   Show units.
-   Identify the fiscal year.
-   Identify whether dollars are nominal or inflation-adjusted.
-   Use consistent scales when comparing districts.
-   Avoid truncated axes when they could materially distort
    interpretation.
-   Provide source information directly below or beside charts.
-   Provide methodology for calculated metrics.
-   Provide accessible text alternatives for important charts.

### District Colors

Use a consistent district mapping throughout the site.

Recommended:

-   Tooele --- Yellow
-   Iron --- Blue
-   Cache --- Teal
-   Box Elder --- Purple

The colors should remain consistent across every chart.

Yellow represents the subject district rather than a positive or
negative judgment.

Use a modern sans-serif with strong numerical readability.

Bold or semibold.

Large homepage headlines should use approximately:

-   Desktop: 56--72px
-   Tablet: 44--56px
-   Mobile: 36--44px

### Body

Approximately:

-   Desktop: 17--18px
-   Mobile: 16px
-   Line height: 1.5--1.7

### Data

Use tabular/monospaced numerals when available.

Financial values should be easy to scan:

`$418.6M`

`$17,035`

**Why is spending increasing?**

**How much does debt cost taxpayers?**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 15. Investigation Page Template

Each investigation should follow a consistent structure.

## Headline

Clear and factual.

Avoid:

> "District wastes millions"

Prefer:

> "Administrative spending increased 42% over ten years. What changed?"

## Executive Summary

Three to five bullet points.

## What We Found

Present the core evidence.

## The Numbers

Interactive charts and tables.

## Why It Matters

Explain the taxpayer relevance without advocating a conclusion.

## How Tooele Compares

Peer-district comparison.

## What We Don't Know

Explicitly identify limitations.

## Possible Explanations

List documented explanations and clearly identify hypotheses.

## Sources & Methodology

Complete source list.

## Data Download

Allow users to download the underlying dataset where possible.

# 16. "Questions Worth Asking" Component

This should become a signature design element.

Use a yellow accent box:

> **QUESTION WORTH ASKING**

Then state a neutral research question.

Example:

> Transportation spending increased faster than enrollment over the last
> five years. What factors explain the difference?

This is preferable to making an unsupported conclusion.

# 17. Source Transparency

Every chart should have a small source line.

Example:

> Source: Tooele County School District FY2026 Final Budget; analysis by
> Tooele School Finance. Updated October 2026.

Clicking the source should open the underlying document or source page.

For derived calculations:

> Calculation: Total General Fund Expenditures ÷ Average Daily
> Membership. See methodology.

# 24. Responsive Design

Mobile users should receive the same information hierarchy.

Prioritize:

1.  Key metric
2.  Finding
3.  Chart
4.  Explanation
5.  Source

Charts should support horizontal scrolling when necessary rather than
becoming unreadably small.

Tables should provide mobile-friendly column selection.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 25. Accessibility

Target WCAG 2.2 AA.

Requirements:

-   Sufficient contrast
-   Keyboard navigation
-   Visible focus states
-   Semantic headings
-   Screen-reader labels
-   Chart text alternatives
-   Never rely on color alone
-   Accessible tables
-   Responsive text sizing

Yellow should never be used as the sole indicator of an important
distinction.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 26. Dark Mode

Dark mode is encouraged for the data explorer but should not be required
for the main public-facing pages.

Dark dashboard:

-   Background: `#08111F`
-   Surface: `#111827`
-   Primary text: `#F8FAFC`
-   Secondary text: `#CBD5E1`
-   Accent: `#FACC15`

Charts should be tested independently for contrast.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 27. Design Principles

## 1. Evidence over emotion

The visual design should encourage curiosity, not outrage.

## 2. Numbers before adjectives

Show the number before describing what it means.

## 3. Questions before conclusions

Where evidence is incomplete, frame the issue as a question.

## 4. Local but independent

Use Tooele imagery without adopting the district's branding.

## 5. Data should be approachable

A taxpayer should understand the first chart without knowing government
accounting.

## 6. Transparency applies to the project itself

Show methodology, sources, calculations, and limitations.

## 7. No visual manipulation

Charts must accurately represent the underlying data.

## 8. Consistency

The same metric should look and behave the same everywhere on the site.

# 30. Overall Design Recommendation

The final experience should sit visually between:

**Investigative journalism**

and

**Modern financial/data dashboard**

with a strong local civic identity.

The key visual idea is:

> **Dark navy + white space + yellow highlights + large numbers +
> rigorous charts + restrained local photography.**

The site should make a taxpayer want to ask:

> "What's happening here?"

and then give them enough evidence to investigate the answer themselves.

The design should never tell the taxpayer what conclusion to reach.
