Students

How many students, and how much per student?

Per-student figures divide spending or revenue by October 1 enrollment. Tooele’s enrollment includes students in the district’s online program, which was large for several years and then shrank sharply in the fall of 2023. That change alone moves every per-student figure, so the two are shown together here.

Tooele’s spending per student rose from $9,051 in FY2023 to $13,576 in FY2024, the same year its online program fell from 8,882 to 263 students. How much did the online program cost to run, and how much of the district’s spending did it account for before and after?
Revenue

Where does the money come from?

Utah school districts are funded by three main sources. Local revenue is mostly property tax. State revenue flows largely through the Minimum School Program, which uses a per-student funding formula. Federal revenue is mostly tied to specific programs, such as special education and services for low-income students.

Where does the money go?

Spending is reported by function — the purpose of the spending, such as instruction or transportation — under Utah’s uniform chart of accounts. That makes Tooele’s categories directly comparable with other Utah districts.

The district’s funds

Government money is kept in separate funds, each with its own legal purpose. Money in one fund generally can’t be spent on another fund’s purpose without a documented transfer. That’s why the total budget is less useful than looking fund by fund.

Reserves

How much does the district keep in reserve?

At the end of each year, money left in the General Fund becomes its fund balance. Some of it is legally restricted or committed by the board — for example, Utah allows districts to commit funds for economic stabilization. The unassigned portion is available for any purpose. A reserve protects against revenue shortfalls; how large it should be is a policy question this site does not answer. Comparing it with annual spending puts districts of different sizes on the same scale.

Pay

Teacher and administrator pay

Most school spending is salaries and benefits. These figures come from the compensation reports districts file with the State Auditor. Teacher pay covers classroom teachers matched to a course they taught; administrator figures cover every employee the Auditor classifies as an administrator. Both include salary and benefits.

Tooele’s administrator headcount grew from 44 in FY2016 to 93 in FY2025, while Iron, Cache, and Box Elder each added 6 to 7. Tooele’s median administrator pay is at or below its peers’. What positions were added, and are they titled or classified differently than in peer districts?
Debt & Bonds

What does the district owe?

Utah school districts borrow for construction by issuing general obligation bonds, which require voter approval. Bonds are repaid over many years through a dedicated debt-service property tax levy. The total cost to taxpayers includes interest, not just the amount borrowed.
