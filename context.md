# Utah Public School Financial Accountability Project

## Mission

Provide Utah taxpayers with an independent, factual,
accessible analysis of Utah School districts finances,
taxation, spending, debt, staffing, enrollment, capital projects,
and educational outcomes.

The project's primary purposes are:

1. Government accountability
2. Taxpayer education and advocacy
3. Financial transparency
4. Identification and investigation of potentially inefficient,
   unusual, unexplained, or questionable spending

The project is politically neutral and does not advocate for or
against political candidates, parties, school board members, ballot
measures, tax increases, bonds, or specific policy outcomes.

## Central Research Question

Are Utah school districts using taxpayer resources efficiently
relative to:

- its educational responsibilities;
- student enrollment and enrollment growth;
- inflation;
- historical district performance;
- other comparable Utah school districts;
- available state and federal funding; and
- measurable educational outcomes?

The project must not assume that the answer is yes or no.

## Political Neutrality

The project must remain politically neutral.

The project may:

- investigate government spending;
- identify unusual financial trends;
- compare government entities;
- question unexplained spending;
- analyze tax burdens;
- identify discrepancies;
- evaluate documented claims;
- present evidence that may be unfavorable to the district.

The project must not:

- advocate for political candidates;
- advocate for political parties;
- assume misconduct;
- characterize spending as wasteful without evidence;
- infer intent from financial data;
- use inflammatory or partisan language;
- rank political actors;
- present advocacy claims as facts.

## Investigative Standard

The project should actively identify:

- unusually rapid expenditure growth;
- spending that significantly differs from peer districts;
- unexplained changes;
- unusual fund transfers;
- persistent budget variances;
- rapidly growing administrative costs;
- unusual contracting or procurement patterns;
- debt and interest costs;
- capital-project cost overruns;
- unexplained changes in staffing;
- discrepancies between public statements and financial records.

A finding should be treated as a research question rather than proof
of wrongdoing.

Example:

BAD:
"The district wasted $5 million."

GOOD:
"Transportation spending increased $5 million over the period.
The available financial records do not explain the increase. The
project will investigate the underlying expenditures and compare the
increase with enrollment, fuel costs, staffing, and peer districts."

## Financial Analysis Standards

- Nominal dollars
- Inflation-adjusted dollars
- Per-student spending
- Per-student revenue
- Percentage of total spending
- Percentage of total revenue
- Year-over-year change
- Five-year change
- Ten-year change

Dollar comparisons across years should consider inflation.

Spending comparisons should consider enrollment.

## Efficiency Analysis

The project should distinguish between:

1. Financial efficiency
2. Operational efficiency
3. Educational effectiveness

Lower spending is not automatically better.

Higher spending is not automatically evidence of poor management.

Efficiency analysis should consider both resources consumed and
measurable outputs/outcomes.

## Required Distinction

Every significant finding should distinguish:

FACT:
What the source explicitly reports.

CALCULATION:
What the project calculates from source data.

INTERPRETATION:
What the data appears to indicate.

HYPOTHESIS:
A possible explanation requiring further investigation.

OPINION:
An interpretation attributed to an identifiable person or organization.

The AI must not present interpretations or hypotheses as facts.

# Project Scope Update

## Project Name / Scope

The project is expanding from a Tooele County School District-focused financial transparency project into a broader **Utah Schools public-data and accountability research project**.

**Tooele County School District (TCSD) remains the initial case study and prototype.**

The long-term objective is to build a reusable data, research, and publishing framework capable of analyzing Utah public school districts statewide.

### Project progression

1. **Phase 1 — Tooele Case Study**
   - Build a comprehensive financial and operational profile of Tooele County School District.
   - Develop the site's visual identity, methodology, data model, and investigative framework.
   - Identify interesting financial, operational, taxation, staffing, enrollment, debt, capital, and educational-outcome questions.
   - Validate the methodology against primary government records.

2. **Phase 2 — Peer District Comparison**
   - Initially compare Tooele with:
     - Iron County School District
     - Cache County School District
     - Box Elder School District
   - Expand comparison metrics and identify meaningful peer groups based on enrollment, geography, growth, demographics, and district characteristics.

3. **Phase 3 — Utah Statewide Dataset**
   - Expand normalized datasets to all Utah school districts and charter schools where appropriate.
   - Create a statewide school-finance data explorer.
   - Develop district profiles using the same standardized metrics.
   - Enable statewide trend and peer analysis.

4. **Phase 4 — Investigative Research**
   - Use the statewide dataset to identify unusual trends, outliers, spending patterns, budget variances, compensation patterns, debt activity, capital expenditures, and other areas warranting further investigation.
   - Return to primary documents before publishing substantive findings.

It should:

# Editorial Standard for Investigations

Use a consistent structure:

### What we found
Documented facts and calculations.

### Why it stands out
Explain the numerical or historical anomaly.

Explicitly identify missing information and limitations.

### Possible explanations
Present reasonable explanations without assuming intent.

### Questions worth asking
Identify questions that taxpayers, board members, journalists, or administrators may reasonably want answered.

### Sources
Link directly to primary documentation.

### Methodology
Explain calculations, assumptions, normalization, and limitations.

### Data
Provide downloadable or reproducible underlying data whenever practical.
